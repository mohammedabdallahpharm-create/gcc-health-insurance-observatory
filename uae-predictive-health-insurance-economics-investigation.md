# Beyond Claims — UAE Predictive Health and Insurance Economics

**Record type:** Strategic investigation / cross-signal synthesis  
**Market:** United Arab Emirates, with Abu Dhabi as the primary implementation case  
**Research cut-off:** 27 November 2026  
**Version:** Working investigation v0.1  
**Status:** Evidence-backed working synthesis; operational connections and economic outcomes remain partly unverified  
**Related Observatory records:** [UAE Healthcare Strategic Context](./uae-healthcare-strategic-context.md); [Signal 010 — Patient Data Flow and Decision Power in UAE Healthcare](./signal-010-patient-data-flow-decision-power-uae-healthcare.md); [2026 Strategic Actions Register](./2026-strategic-actions-register.md)

## 1. Research question

**Is the UAE connecting national disease intelligence, predictive population-health capabilities and health-insurance financing in ways that can move healthcare from treating complications toward anticipating health needs and preventing avoidable expenditure?**

This investigation tests the relationship between three layers:

1. **National health intelligence:** Emirates Health, the Enterprise Data Warehouse (EDW) and the national Disease Registry System Bayan.
2. **Predictive population-health intelligence:** Abu Dhabi's Population Health Intelligence (PHI) platform and related prevention capabilities.
3. **Healthcare financing and insurance economics:** clinical costing, Shafafiya, provider reimbursement, insurer/TPA operations and health-insurance market economics.

The investigation does not assume that these systems are technically integrated, that insurers can access predictive health data, or that prevention has already reduced claims expenditure.

## 2. Executive assessment

The public evidence supports three developments occurring in parallel:

- MoHAP presented Emirates Health's EDW and Bayan in February 2026 as a national repository and disease-intelligence capability, with data integration across public and private stakeholders, more than 10 disease registries, more than 45 dashboards and more than 300 health indicators.
- DoH Abu Dhabi and Microsoft announced a PHI framework in April 2025 and unveiled the platform in October 2025. Its stated design combines clinical, behavioural, environmental and demographic data in a digital twin, with Predict and Prevent & Act pillars. Obesity and cancer were identified as initial focus areas.
- Abu Dhabi has moved beyond a general value-based-care ambition into a formal clinical-costing programme: DoH says the Clinical Costing and Value-Based Funding Framework project was completed in August 2024; clinical costing is mandatory for applicable direct-patient-care facilities; and Clinical Costing Standard V2 and related technical documentation were published in February 2026. Shafafiya is identified in the technical documentation as the platform for both claims transactions and clinical-costing submissions.

**Working conclusion:** the UAE has visible components of a data-enabled, prevention-oriented health system and an increasingly structured cost-information framework. The public evidence reviewed does **not** yet establish a fully integrated national predictive-insurance operating model, routine insurer use of PHI predictions, a formal payer reimbursement mechanism tied to PHI-generated interventions, or independently measured reductions in avoidable claims or total cost of care.

The strongest evidenced financial bridge in this review is Abu Dhabi's patient-level clinical-costing infrastructure—not a proven connection from Bayan or PHI into insurer adjudication or payment.

## 3. System map: three layers, distinct mandates

### Layer A — Emirates Health / EDW / Bayan

**Institution:** Ministry of Health and Prevention (MoHAP), under the national Emirates Health umbrella with federal and local health authorities.

**Documented capability:**
- A centralised national health-data repository, with direct electronic integration described across public- and private-sector data partners.
- More than 10 disease registries, including cancer, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, mental health and disability.
- More than 45 interactive dashboards and more than 300 health indicators, as reported by MoHAP on 11 February 2026.
- Data coverage described across healthcare services, priority diseases, vital statistics, workforce/facility resources, births, deaths, immunisation and communicable diseases.
- MoHAP describes the use of data science and AI to generate predictive models for policy and planning.

**What this establishes:** national-level disease and health-system intelligence capability, including an announced predictive-analytics direction.

**What it does not establish:** individual-level clinical prediction in routine care; a live operational interface with Abu Dhabi PHI; insurer or TPA access to identifiable Bayan data; or measured effects on claims.

Primary source: [MoHAP — Emirates Health Platform showcases EDW and Bayan (11 February 2026)](https://mohap.gov.ae/en/w/emirates-health-platform-continues-participation-at-world-health-expo-2026)

### Layer B — Abu Dhabi Population Health Intelligence

**Institution:** Department of Health — Abu Dhabi (DoH), in collaboration with Microsoft.

**Development chronology:**
- **21 April 2025:** DoH and Microsoft signed an MoU to co-develop a Population Health Intelligence framework.
- **15 October 2025:** DoH unveiled the PHI platform at GITEX Global 2025.
- **8 February 2026:** DoH included PHI among the AI-driven initiatives presented under its prevention-led, data-enabled health approach at WHX 2026.

**Documented design:**
- A digital twin of Abu Dhabi's healthcare ecosystem.
- Intended integration of clinical, behavioural, environmental and demographic data.
- **Predict:** identify anomalies, emerging risks and trends for early investigation.
- **Prevent & Act:** develop context-aware recommendations, simulate potential intervention impacts and support coordinated responses.
- Initial focus areas announced at launch: obesity and cancer.

**What this establishes:** a formally announced and unveiled emirate-level predictive population-health platform, with a stated prevention and intervention architecture.

**What it does not establish:** the exact production datasets and refresh cycles; current coverage of residents or providers; a direct technical link to Bayan; routine use by insurers/TPAs; or independently measured clinical and financial outcomes.

Primary sources:
- [DoH — MoU with Microsoft to create PHI framework (21 April 2025)](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/doh-signs-a-memorandum-of-understanding-with-microsoft-to-create-an-ai-powered-phif-)
- [Abu Dhabi Media Office — PHI platform unveiled at GITEX Global (15 October 2025)](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/department-of-health-abu-dhabi-unveils-worlds-1st-ai-powered-population-health-intelligence-platform-at-gitex-global-2025/)
- [DoH — Prevention-led, data and AI-enabled approach at WHX 2026 (8 February 2026)](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/world-health-expo-2026)

### Layer C — Insurance economics, claims and clinical costing

**Institutions and mechanisms:** DoH Abu Dhabi, providers, payers, Shafafiya, insurers and TPAs; CBUAE for federal insurance-market reporting and supervision.

#### C1. Clinical Costing and Value-Based Funding

DoH states that it has pursued value-based healthcare principles since 2015. Its 15-month Clinical Costing and Value-Based Funding Framework project was completed in August 2024 after stakeholder engagement. A key component is the collection of annual patient-level cost data from providers.

DoH states that clinical costing is mandatory across the Abu Dhabi healthcare market for facilities providing direct patient care, subject to specified exceptions. The 2026 Road Map and Clinical Costing Standard V2 were published in February 2026.

The DoH technical document states that:
- providers submit patient-level clinical-cost data through the specified data-collection process;
- Shafafiya is Abu Dhabi's transaction exchange platform for claims data between providers, payers and government entities; and
- Shafafiya is also used as the platform for clinical-costing data submissions and validation.

The technical document describes the costing work as aligned with DoH's goal of establishing a value-based funding model in which reimbursement can be informed by service quality, efficiency and cost of care.

**Evidence boundary:** the costing framework and data-collection infrastructure are evidenced. The public materials reviewed do not prove that all provider reimbursement is already outcome-based, that PHI risk scores determine payment, or that a payer contract has been changed because of PHI predictions.

Primary sources:
- [DoH — Abu Dhabi Clinical Costing Road Map](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/Shafafiya/Clinical-Costing)
- [DoH — Clinical Costing Road Map 2026 / Standard V2](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/Shafafiya/Clinical-Costing/tap2026)
- [DoH — Clinical Cost Data Collection Technical Document](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/Feature/shafifya/Costing/roadMap/Clinical-Costing-Road-Map---Cost-Data-Collection-Technical-Document-SY2-v100.ashx)

#### C2. UAE health-insurance market scale

The CBUAE Financial Stability Report 2025 reports:
- UAE health-insurance gross written premiums of **AED 36.4 billion in 2025**, up **16.3% year-on-year**.
- Health insurance represented **48.7%** of total UAE insurance gross written premiums in 2025.
- Health-insurance gross claims paid were **AED 26.2 billion in 2025**, as shown in the report's insurance-class breakdown.

These figures establish the scale and economic significance of the insurance segment. They do not establish the amount of avoidable expenditure, the cost of chronic disease alone, or the savings achievable through prevention.

Primary source: [CBUAE — Financial Stability Report 2025, Insurance Sector Assessment](https://centralbank.ae/media/t1jnwh13/fsr-2025-en-3.pdf)

## 4. Adjacent evidence: prevention and continuous health signals

These initiatives reinforce the prevention direction but should not be misrepresented as proof of insurance integration.

### E01 — Wearable-data research partnership

On 8 May 2026, DoH Abu Dhabi and ŌURA announced a joint research programme to study how continuous wearable data could advance preventive health, initially focusing on women's health and cardiometabolic risk.

**Interpretation:** continuous data collected between clinical encounters may add earlier signals to population-health research and intervention design.

**Boundary:** this is a research partnership announcement. It does not establish routine payer access to wearable data, insurer pricing use, or demonstrated claims savings.

Source: [DoH — DoH and ŌURA partner to advance preventive health (8 May 2026)](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/doh-and-oura-partner-to-advance-preventive-health-in-abu-dhabi)

### E02 — Abu Dhabi Biobank

On 3 April 2026, DoH announced the inauguration of the Abu Dhabi Biobank through a strategic partnership with M42, connecting biological samples with genomic, lifestyle and clinical data to support earlier detection and personalised care.

**Interpretation:** this expands the potential research base for precision health and risk understanding.

**Boundary:** the announcement does not establish insurer access to identifiable genomic data, underwriting use, or a change to covered benefits.

Source: [DoH — Abu Dhabi Biobank inaugurated (3 April 2026)](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/doh-inaugurates-abu-dhabi-biobank)

## 7. Economic mechanism to test

A plausible future pathway is:

1. **Population intelligence:** disease registries and health-system data identify trends and population-level burden.
2. **Risk identification:** predictive models identify groups or locations that may benefit from earlier investigation.
3. **Intervention design:** public-health authorities and providers design screening, prevention or chronic-disease programmes.
4. **Financing decision:** government programmes, employers, insurers or other authorised funders decide whether and how to finance the intervention.
5. **Care delivery:** providers deliver the intervention under defined clinical and data-governance arrangements.
6. **Cost and outcome measurement:** clinical costing, utilisation, claims and outcome measures are compared over an appropriate period.
7. **Funding adjustment:** evidence may inform future benefit design, provider purchasing, pricing or value-based funding.

This is an analytical model, not a description of a currently verified end-to-end UAE workflow.

### Economic tests

To claim that prevention creates savings, analysis would need to measure at least:
- programme cost per eligible member or population segment;
- intervention uptake and completion;
- disease progression and complication rates;
- emergency visits and avoidable admissions;
- claims frequency and severity, with consistent definitions;
- total cost of care and patient outcomes;
- comparison groups or credible counterfactuals;
- follow-up duration sufficient to capture delayed effects;
- insurer/member retention and payer changes where relevant.

A fall in claims alone is not proof of prevention impact. It may reflect changes in enrolment, benefits, coding, provider networks, utilisation, prices or reporting.

## 8. Insurance economics: strategic implications and hypotheses

### H1 — Prevention could become a purchasing and benefit-design question

If interventions demonstrate clinical value and acceptable cost-effectiveness, insurers, employers and public funders may have reasons to finance selected preventive services. Whether they do so depends on benefits, regulation, contracts, member continuity and the distribution of long-term savings.

### H2 — Claims data could become a feedback mechanism for prevention

Claims histories can potentially help identify utilisation patterns, treatment gaps and avoidable escalation. This requires lawful access, data quality, clinical context and safeguards against treating claims data as a complete representation of health status.

### H3 — TPAs may extend from transaction administration toward care and analytics coordination

TPAs could potentially support programme eligibility workflows, provider coordination, claims monitoring and reporting. This is an opportunity hypothesis, not evidence that UAE TPAs currently operate PHI-linked prevention programmes.

### H4 — Provider costing could strengthen the evidence base for purchasing

Patient-level costing can help compare the resources used to deliver care. Combined with quality and outcome measures, it may support more informed pricing and funding. Cost data alone is not a measure of value: outcomes and case-mix adjustment matter.

### H5 — The payer incentive may be weaker than the population-health incentive

Prevention may generate benefits over multiple years, while insurance contracts and member enrolment can change annually. This can create a mismatch between who funds an intervention now and who benefits from lower future costs. The size and practical significance of this issue in UAE schemes requires market-specific evidence.

## 9. Data governance and jurisdictional boundaries

- Clinical health-information exchange is not the same as insurance-claims exchange.
- MOHAP's public Riayati information describes access for authorised clinical users such as doctors, nurses and allied-health clinicians. This does not establish general insurer/TPA access.
- DoH's PHI announcement describes patient-data confidentiality and privacy safeguards. The specific data-sharing permissions, controller/processor roles, legal bases and access roles for each dataset require separate documentation.
- Abu Dhabi's clinical-costing framework is emirate-specific. Do not generalise its standards or reimbursement implications to Dubai or all UAE markets.
- Bayan is a national MoHAP-led data initiative; PHI is a DoH Abu Dhabi platform. Their institutional relationship must not be inferred from their shared participation under the Emirates Health umbrella.

## 11. Competing interpretations

**Interpretation A — Emerging integrated learning-health model:** national disease intelligence, emirate-level prediction, clinical costing and financing mechanisms may gradually form a learning system that improves prevention and resource allocation.

**Interpretation B — Parallel programmes with limited operational connection:** the platforms may remain institutionally and technically separate, with data sharing, payer participation and funding decisions developing at different speeds.

**Interpretation C — Strong public-health intelligence, slow insurance adaptation:** government systems may advance prediction and prevention while commercial insurance remains primarily organised around annual benefits, networks, authorisation, claims and reimbursement.

The current public evidence is consistent with all three. More operational and outcome evidence is needed to distinguish them.

## 12. Working conclusion

The UAE is building important foundations for a more predictive and prevention-oriented healthcare system. MoHAP's EDW/Bayan strengthens national disease and health-system intelligence. Abu Dhabi's PHI introduces an explicitly predictive and intervention-oriented population-health architecture. Abu Dhabi's clinical-costing programme and Shafafiya create a concrete mechanism for collecting patient-level cost information and supporting the development of value-based funding.

**The bridge between these layers is not yet proven.** The available public evidence does not establish a direct Bayan-to-PHI data connection, routine PHI-to-insurer data sharing, payer-funded interventions driven by predictive outputs, or measured reductions in insurance claims attributable to prevention.

The most defensible thesis is therefore:

> The UAE is assembling the institutional, data and costing foundations for a more predictive healthcare economy. The next strategic test is whether these capabilities become connected to real financing decisions, provider incentives and measurable patient outcomes.

For health insurance, the future opportunity may be to move beyond paying for episodes of care toward helping finance and measure interventions that prevent avoidable deterioration—while preserving clinical judgement, patient privacy and fair access.
