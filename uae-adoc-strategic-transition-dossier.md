# ADOC Strategic Transition Dossier — Abu Dhabi, UAE

**Record type:** Regulatory / reimbursement transformation dossier  
**Research cut-off:** 11 November 2026  
**Geographic scope:** Abu Dhabi emirate; implications for the wider UAE are hypotheses to test, not assumed spillovers.  
**Status:** Working research record v0.1  
**Purpose:** Document the ADOC reform, the public-sector implementation sequence, private-sector and ecosystem moves that may support or interact with it, and the pathways through which it could reshape the health-insurance market in 2027.

## Central research question

**How could the Abu Dhabi Outpatient Classification (ADOC) change the distribution of payment, operational responsibility, information requirements and bargaining power among healthcare providers, insurers and third-party administrators (TPAs) from 2027 onward?**

A second question governs the ecosystem scan:

**Which proactive strategic steps did public and private organisations take in 2026 that prepare them for, complement, or may be independent of the ADOC transition?**

Do not claim causality merely because an initiative and ADOC occurred in the same year. Classify each action as:
- **Direct ADOC action:** explicitly required, issued or organised as part of ADOC.
- **Adjacent enabling capability:** may help organisations handle structured data, claims, audit, provider networks or operational change, but no direct ADOC link is evidenced.
- **Independent market move:** a strategic action that may interact with the new reimbursement environment but has no demonstrated ADOC connection.
- **Hypothesis:** a proposed impact pathway requiring further evidence.

## 1. What ADOC is

The Abu Dhabi Outpatient Classification is a patient-centred framework for classifying eligible outpatient care. The Department of Health — Abu Dhabi describes it as part of the emirate's transition from fee-for-service toward value-based, case-mix funding.

ADOC changes the unit of classification from individual line-item activity to the outpatient service event. Each eligible event is assigned the single class that best reflects the predominant nature of care; diagnostic inputs are reported as linked information rather than treated automatically as separate service events.

The framework uses four series:
- **P — Interventional Procedures**
- **C — Specialist Consultations**
- **S — Support Services**
- **D — Diagnostic Services**

The public ADOC hub identifies hospital outpatient departments, outpatient clinics, eligible telemedicine, specialist consultations, interventional procedures, eligible support services and linked diagnostic reporting as in scope. It lists admitted care, emergency care, daycare, home care, GP/family medicine, dental services, renal dialysis procedures and IVF/ART procedures as out of scope. The current Definitions Manual is the controlling reference for scope and classification.

**Important interpretation:** ADOC is not simply a new code list. It changes how an eligible outpatient encounter is represented, counted, submitted and reimbursed. The actual financial effect depends on the final rules, price arrangements, service mix, implementation and provider/insurer behaviour.

## 2. Regulatory and implementation chronology

### 2024 — Earlier regulatory foundation
DoH Circular 226/2024 is cited by the 2026 circular as the earlier step in the continuing reimbursement reform. The 2026 transition is therefore not a standalone 2026 announcement; it follows an earlier design and regulatory process.

### 2024–2025 — Design and co-development
The current DoH ADOC hub describes 2024 as the design phase and 2025 as co-development, including clinical advisory input and market testing. This is DoH's own programme chronology.

### 14 May 2026 — Circular 76/2026
DoH issued Circular 76/2026 to:
- licensed healthcare service providers operating in health insurance;
- health-insurance companies licensed by DoH; and
- licensed health-insurance claims-management companies / TPAs.

The circular states that ADOC is the next phase of implementing a payment method for outpatient care and sets mandatory shadow-billing submission from 1 July 2026.

The stated aims of shadow billing are to test stakeholder system and procedural readiness, standardise claims submission, improve classification and calculation accuracy, and resolve problems before official implementation.

The circular says May–June 2026 would include further information on draft pricing and orientation workshops. It requires shadow-billing data to be submitted through Shafafiya under the ADOC Definitions Manual and approved calculation rules.

It sets 1 January 2027 as the transition to ADOC as the primary reimbursement mechanism for outpatient services in Abu Dhabi. It also explicitly says shadow billing must not disrupt the usual claims settlement, payment or approval processes during the shadow phase.

**Evidence status:** Formal regulatory instruction.  
**Primary source:** https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/976B3ECBEBD240558CF0F900052AA896.ashx

### Current documentation and version-control issue — 11 November 2026
The public ADOC programme hub identifies Version 1.4 as the current release for the shadow-billing phase and describes Version 1.5 and Version 1.6 as planned controlled updates.

However, the DoH Prices page lists ADOC v1.5 materials, including a Classification Mapping Tool, Counting Rules, Definitions Manual, draft Claims & Adjudication Rules and Price List, alongside September 2026 shadow-billing rules and materials.

**Unresolved discrepancy:** The two public pages do not present the same release status. Do not state that v1.5 is definitively the operative release until DoH's controlled release materials or authenticated technical guidance confirm it. Track the release centre and Prices page for updates.

## 3. Proactive strategic actions by public-sector actors

### PS-01 — DoH created a staged transition rather than an immediate switch
**Action:** Design in 2024, co-development and market testing in 2025, then mandatory shadow billing in 2026 before the stated 2027 transition.

**Classification:** Direct ADOC action.  
**Strategic mechanism:** A controlled transition period allows providers, insurers and TPAs to test classification, data submission, counting, claims and adjudication workflows while existing payment processes continue.

**2027 question:** What issues surfaced during shadow billing, which rules changed between versions, and how were unresolved implementation problems handled?

### PS-02 — DoH made readiness a shared obligation across the payer-provider-administrator chain
**Action:** Circular 76/2026 explicitly includes licensed outpatient providers, health insurers and TPAs in mandatory shadow billing, excluding dental providers and pharmacies from this phase.

**Classification:** Direct ADOC action.  
**Strategic mechanism:** Readiness cannot be confined to hospitals. Payer and TPA systems must be able to receive, validate, interpret and process the new event-level classification and associated data.

**2027 question:** Which organisations completed readiness, which required remediation, and how were operational responsibilities divided where claims administration is outsourced?

### PS-03 — DoH provided structured technical and policy channels
**Action:** The ADOC hub directs organisations to current controlled documents, implementation-contact registration, shadow-billing preparation and a structured query route. Circular 76/2026 also identifies Health System Financing Regulation as the contact point.

**Classification:** Direct ADOC action.  
**Strategic mechanism:** Creates a controlled process for clarifying definitions, scope, counting and implementation questions.

**2027 question:** Are clarifications and release changes frequent? Do they indicate recurring ambiguity in classification or operational design?

## 4. Private-sector and ecosystem actions during 2026

### EC-01 — Daman / PureHealth launched AI-powered document intelligence
**Date:** 18 September 2026  
**Actors:** Daman; PureHealth; PureCS  
**Action:** Publicly announced launch of an AI-powered document-processing solution intended to process more than 60 million documents annually for more than three million members. The announcement states targets/expected outcomes including conversion of more than 80% of documents into structured digital data, reduction of processing time from days to minutes and more than 50% less manual rework. It uses large language models, intelligent classification and structured data extraction and is integrated into policy-related workflows, with future AI enhancements including claims and prior-authorisation processing.

**Classification:** Adjacent enabling capability; not proven to be an ADOC-specific project.  
**Evidence status:** Official launch announcement. The quantitative performance statements are company/government-announcement claims; independent outcome validation was not located in this research pass.  
**Potential ADOC relevance:** Structured data, classification, validation and reduced manual rework could help an insurer handle more structured claims and supporting documents. But document intelligence is not the same as ADOC service-event classification, nor does it prove ADOC adjudication is automated.

**2027 tests:** Is ADOC data mapped into Daman workflows? Are outpatient claims classified or adjudicated using ADOC-specific logic? Are turnaround times, error rates or rework measured before and after? What human review and exception processes remain?

**Source:** https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/purehealth-launches-ai-powered-solution-to-enhance-access-to-healthcare-services-for-daman-members/

### EC-02 — GlobeMed Gulf and Orient Insurance entered a TPA partnership
**Date:** January 2026  
**Actors:** GlobeMed Gulf; Orient Insurance  
**Action:** Agreement to provide enhanced TPA services to Orient's insured members in the UAE. GlobeMed described this as a relaunch of its UAE operations and referred to its proprietary health-insurance management system, cost-containment measures, digital member services and 24/7 customer support.

**Classification:** Independent market move / potentially adjacent operational capability. No direct ADOC connection is stated in the announcement.  
**Evidence status:** Corporate announcement of agreement; operational transfer scope, covered lives, implementation milestones and performance outcomes were not established in this research pass.

**Potential ADOC relevance:** A new insurer–TPA operating relationship creates an opportunity to examine how delegated claims operations, systems configuration, coding expertise and accountability are organised ahead of reimbursement reform.

**2027 tests:** Which Orient portfolios are administered by GlobeMed? Was the transition completed? Did the contract specify ADOC readiness, reporting, data quality, service levels or responsibility for classification disputes?

**Source:** https://www.globemedgulf.com/en/newsroom/globemed-gulf--orient-insurance-partner-to-deliver-differentiated-tpa-services-to-orients-insured-members-in-the-uae

### EC-03 — ADI Foundation and Apeiro tested a blockchain-based claims-verification layer
**Date:** 10 February 2026  
**Actors:** ADI Foundation; Apeiro  
**Action:** The organisations reported completion of a first phase testing claims-verification workflows on the ADI Chain testnet. The proposed model records time-stamped digital fingerprints of claim actions to support traceability and auditability; the announcement says patient or medical data is not stored on-chain and existing hospital/insurer systems remain unchanged. The next phase was described as integration into real-world healthcare-insurance environments.

**Classification:** Adjacent enabling capability / pilot-stage ecosystem initiative. No direct ADOC link or production deployment was established.  
**Evidence status:** Testnet validation reported by the partners; production deployment not evidenced in the cited announcement.

**Potential ADOC relevance:** ADOC's structured event and claims workflows could increase the importance of traceable classification, submission, correction and adjudication histories. A tamper-evident audit layer is one possible complementary approach, but it is not part of ADOC itself.

**2027 tests:** Was a live insurer/provider integration completed? Which organisations participated? Was the system used in Abu Dhabi? Did it handle ADOC-coded events? Were audit, dispute or fraud outcomes independently measured?

**Source:** https://www.wam.ae/en/article/byo5gt8-adi-foundation-apeiro-partner-advance-healthcare

### EC-04 — GlobeMed Gulf and Mubadala Health Dubai signed a provider-access cooperation agreement
**Date:** 6 July 2026  
**Actors:** GlobeMed Gulf; Mubadala Health Dubai / M42  
**Action:** Strategic cooperation to expand eligible insured members' access to diagnostics, day surgery, paediatrics and rehabilitation services within Mubadala Health Dubai.

**Classification:** Independent provider-network move; potentially relevant to outpatient service pathways, but no direct ADOC connection is stated.  
**Evidence status:** Agreement and intended access expansion announced. Actual utilisation, contracted prices, eligibility by insurer/product and outcomes were not quantified in the announcement.

**Potential ADOC relevance:** If outpatient service-event reimbursement changes, provider groups and benefits managers may reassess which outpatient pathways, service bundles and network arrangements are attractive. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated response.

**2027 tests:** Which insurer/TPA plans can access the services? What are the contracted rates and referral rules? Did day-surgery or outpatient pathways shift? Are claims and service-event classifications mapped to ADOC?

**Source:** https://m42.ae/media-resources/news/globemed-gulf-and-mubadala-health-dubai-sign-strategic-cooperation-agreement-to-enhance-healthcare-access-for-insured-members/

## 5. What these moves do — and do not — establish

The evidence currently supports three distinct layers:

1. **Regulatory transition is real and mandatory:** DoH has set a staged process involving providers, insurers and TPAs, with a stated 1 January 2027 primary-reimbursement transition.
2. **Some market participants are strengthening adjacent capabilities:** Daman's AI document intelligence, GlobeMed's insurer/provider partnerships and the ADI–Apeiro testnet project point to activity around structured data, operational efficiency, networks and auditability.
3. **Direct private-sector ADOC readiness remains under-documented:** This research pass has not yet established named ADOC implementation programmes, public readiness disclosures, ADOC-specific system integrations or measured shadow-billing results for individual insurers, TPAs or provider groups.

Do not infer that every AI, blockchain, network or TPA initiative was caused by ADOC. Several may be part of broader strategies already underway.

## 6. 2027 market-shape hypotheses to test

### H1 — Outpatient reimbursement becomes more classification- and event-driven
If ADOC is implemented as scheduled, the service event and its predominant clinical nature become more central to payment representation. This may alter coding, counting, billing and adjudication work.

**Evidence needed:** Final operative rules, price list, actual claims examples, provider payment statements and payer/TPA implementation evidence.

### H2 — Data quality and classification capability become more commercially important
Providers may need stronger clinical documentation and coding controls; insurers and TPAs may need stronger validation, mapping and exception handling.

**Evidence needed:** Rejection/return reasons, resubmission rates, coding queries, audit findings, staffing and system changes.

### H3 — Payer–provider contract economics may be renegotiated
Changes in reimbursement logic could alter the economics of outpatient service lines and the way providers negotiate with payers.

**Evidence needed:** New or amended contracts, rate schedules, service-line margins, network changes and provider financial disclosures.

### H4 — TPA value proposition may shift toward rule interpretation and data operations
If claims become more dependent on event classification, counting and linked diagnostics, TPA capability may be assessed beyond transaction throughput.

**Evidence needed:** Contract SLAs, ADOC-specific roles, system investments, job descriptions, audit and escalation responsibilities.

### H5 — Automation may move from document handling toward structured claims controls
Daman's document intelligence is an adjacent capability, not proof of ADOC automation. It could provide a foundation for structured workflows if ADOC-specific mapping and controls are developed.

**Evidence needed:** Explicit ADOC integration, production workflows, human-review controls and before/after operational metrics.

### H6 — Market impact may be uneven across service lines and provider types
ADOC has defined scope and exclusions. Hospitals, outpatient clinics, specialist centres, telemedicine providers, GPs, pharmacies, dental providers, daycare and other excluded services should not be treated as equally affected.

**Evidence needed:** Scope-specific implementation data and the final authoritative Definitions Manual.

## 7. Research gaps and next investigation

Priority follow-up work:
1. Obtain and compare the controlled ADOC v1.4 and v1.5 documents, including Definitions Manual, Counting Rules, Claims & Adjudication Rules, price list and September shadow-billing materials. Resolve the public-page version discrepancy.
2. Identify named insurers, TPAs and providers participating in ADOC shadow billing, using official evidence where possible.
3. Search company annual/interim reports, investor presentations, procurement notices, job descriptions and technology partner disclosures for ADOC-specific readiness, system changes, training, hiring and implementation budgets.
4. Examine whether GlobeMed–Orient contract scope and operational launch include Abu Dhabi ADOC portfolios.
5. Examine Daman/PureHealth disclosures for ADOC mapping or outpatient claims workflow integration; do not assume it exists.
6. Track the ADI–Apeiro initiative for production deployments and named UAE participants.
7. Collect provider-side evidence on documentation, coding, revenue-cycle redesign, outpatient service mix and contract renegotiation.
8. After 1 January 2027, verify actual implementation, exceptions, delays, payment disputes and measured outcomes rather than treating the scheduled date as proof of successful go-live.

## 8. Working conclusion

ADOC is a structural reimbursement reform, not merely a coding update. Its most direct effect is to make the outpatient service event, its classification and its supporting data more central to reimbursement. The formal transition requires coordinated readiness across providers, insurers and TPAs.

The early 2026 ecosystem evidence shows adjacent investments in AI document processing, insurer–TPA operations, provider access and claims auditability. These moves may become important complements to the new reimbursement environment, but the causal links and actual readiness of individual market participants remain to be demonstrated.

**Working thesis for 2027:** If ADOC is implemented with stable rules and consistent adoption, competition and operational performance may increasingly depend on the ability to classify, document, validate and financially manage outpatient service events — not only on claims-processing volume or network size. This thesis remains provisional until implementation and market-response evidence is collected.
