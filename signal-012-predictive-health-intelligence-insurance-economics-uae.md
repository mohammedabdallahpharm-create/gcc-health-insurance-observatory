# Signal 012 — From Disease Intelligence to Insurance Economics: UAE's Emerging Predictive Health Infrastructure

**Record ID:** UAE-HI-012  
**Market:** United Arab Emirates; Abu Dhabi is the primary implementation case  
**Signal type:** Population-health intelligence / health-data infrastructure / prevention / healthcare financing  
**Primary radar:** Digital Health & AI  
**Connected radars:** reimbursement, payer & TPA, provider, regulatory, market & capital  
**Primary institutions / systems:** MoHAP / Emirates Health / EDW / Bayan; DoH Abu Dhabi / Population Health Intelligence; Shafafiya; UAE insurers and TPAs  
**Observation date:** 27 November 2026  
**Evidence status:** Official announcements and published frameworks establish several relevant capabilities and financing-data mechanisms. Direct technical integration between platforms, routine insurer/TPA use of predictive outputs, and attributable claims or cost reductions are not established by the public evidence reviewed.  
**Confidence:** High for the existence and announced scope of the separate platforms and the Abu Dhabi clinical-costing framework; moderate for the strategic convergence thesis; low / unverified for an integrated predictive-insurance operating model or realised financial outcomes.

## 1. External Signal Detection

The UAE is developing several components that could support a more predictive and prevention-oriented health system:

- **National health intelligence:** MoHAP presented Emirates Health, including the Enterprise Data Warehouse (EDW) and Bayan disease-registry system, in February 2026.
- **Population-health intelligence:** DoH Abu Dhabi and Microsoft announced a Population Health Intelligence (PHI) framework in April 2025; DoH unveiled the platform in October 2025.
- **Clinical costing and financing data:** Abu Dhabi has formalised a clinical-costing framework. DoH technical materials identify Shafafiya as the platform used for claims transactions and clinical-costing submissions.
- **Insurance-market scale:** CBUAE's Financial Stability Report 2025 records AED 36.4 billion in UAE health-insurance gross written premiums and AED 26.2 billion in health-insurance gross claims paid in 2025.

Together, these developments raise a strategic question: can health intelligence, population-risk prediction and clinical-cost information eventually inform prevention, provider performance and insurance economics?

This record documents the components and tests their possible connection. It does not assume that they are already integrated.

## 2. Primary-source evidence

### A. Emirates Health / EDW / Bayan

On 11 February 2026, MoHAP described the Emirates Health platform's EDW and Bayan capabilities, reporting more than 10 disease registries, more than 45 dashboards and more than 300 health indicators. The announcement describes national health-data integration and the use of data science and AI for predictive models supporting policy and planning.

**Evidence boundary:** this establishes the capabilities and direction described by MoHAP. It does not establish a live operational connection to Abu Dhabi PHI, insurer/TPA access to identifiable Bayan data, or predictive outputs being used in claims adjudication or reimbursement.

Source:
- [MoHAP — Emirates Health Platform showcases EDW and Bayan (11 February 2026)](https://mohap.gov.ae/en/w/emirates-health-platform-continues-participation-at-world-health-expo-2026)

### B. Abu Dhabi Population Health Intelligence

DoH and Microsoft signed an MoU to co-develop a PHI framework on 21 April 2025. DoH unveiled the platform on 15 October 2025. Public descriptions identify a digital-twin approach combining clinical, behavioural, environmental and demographic data, with Predict and Prevent & Act pillars. Obesity and cancer were identified as initial focus areas.

DoH also referenced PHI among its AI-driven initiatives at WHX 2026 in February.

**Evidence boundary:** public announcements establish the framework and described platform design. The precise production datasets, coverage, refresh cycles, operational use, direct Bayan integration, payer access and independently measured outcomes remain unverified.

Sources:
- [DoH — MoU with Microsoft to create a PHI framework (21 April 2025)](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/doh-signs-a-memorandum-of-understanding-with-microsoft-to-create-an-ai-powered-phif-)
- [Abu Dhabi Media Office — PHI platform unveiled at GITEX Global (15 October 2025)](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/health/department-of-health-abu-dhabi-unveils-worlds-1st-ai-powered-population-health-intelligence-platform-at-gitex-global-2025/)
- [DoH — Prevention-led, data and AI-enabled approach at WHX 2026 (8 February 2026)](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/news/world-health-expo-2026)

### C. Clinical costing and Shafafiya

DoH states that its Clinical Costing and Value-Based Funding Framework project was completed in August 2024. Its published materials describe mandatory clinical costing for applicable direct-patient-care facilities, patient-level cost-data collection, and the Clinical Costing Standard V2 and related 2026 documentation.

DoH technical documentation identifies Shafafiya as the transaction-exchange platform for claims data between providers, payers and government entities, and as the platform used for clinical-costing data submissions and validation.

**Evidence boundary:** the clinical-costing framework and submission infrastructure are documented. This does not prove that all reimbursement is already outcome-based, that PHI predictions determine payment, or that insurers have changed contracts or adjudication rules because of predictive-health outputs.

Sources:
- [DoH — Abu Dhabi Clinical Costing Road Map](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/Shafafiya/Clinical-Costing)
- [DoH — Clinical Costing Road Map 2026 / Standard V2](https://www.doh.gov.ae/en/Shafafiya/Clinical-Costing/tap2026)
- [DoH — Clinical Cost Data Collection Technical Document](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/Feature/shafifya/Costing/roadMap/Clinical-Costing-Road-Map---Cost-Data-Collection-Technical-Document-SY2-v100.ashx)

### D. Insurance-market economics

The CBUAE Financial Stability Report 2025 reports UAE health-insurance gross written premiums of AED 36.4 billion in 2025, representing 16.3% year-on-year growth and 48.7% of total UAE insurance gross written premiums. The report's insurance-class breakdown records AED 26.2 billion in health-insurance gross claims paid.

These figures establish the segment's financial scale. They do not quantify avoidable expenditure, the share attributable to chronic disease, or savings achievable through prevention.

Source:
- [CBUAE — Financial Stability Report 2025](https://centralbank.ae/media/t1jnfrc3/fsr-2025-en-3.pdf)

## 4. Strategic interpretation

### Finding 1 — The building blocks are visible, but remain institutionally distinct

National disease intelligence, Abu Dhabi population-health intelligence and clinical-costing infrastructure are all documented. The evidence reviewed does not establish that they currently operate as one integrated data or decision system.

### Finding 2 — The strongest evidenced financial bridge is clinical costing

The clearest documented link between health-system information and financing is the DoH clinical-costing framework and its use of Shafafiya for claims and costing submissions. This is a more defensible current connection than claiming that PHI or Bayan already changes insurer reimbursement.

### Finding 3 — Prevention could become relevant to insurance economics, but the causal chain is not yet demonstrated

A possible future pathway is:

**Population and disease signals → risk identification → targeted prevention → changed care pathways and utilisation → measurable provider costs and claims outcomes → potential financing or contracting changes.**

Each arrow requires operational evidence. A predictive score alone does not demonstrate that an intervention occurred, that outcomes improved, or that an insurer captured savings.

## 5. What is established — and what is not

**Established from the sources reviewed**
- MoHAP's February 2026 public description of Emirates Health, EDW and Bayan.
- DoH/Microsoft's PHI framework announcement and DoH's public unveiling of the platform.
- Abu Dhabi's clinical-costing framework and published technical documentation describing Shafafiya's claims and costing roles.
- The scale of the UAE health-insurance market reported by CBUAE for 2025.

**Not established**
- Direct technical integration between Bayan and PHI.
- Routine insurer or TPA access to PHI predictions or identifiable disease-registry data.
- A PHI-driven claims adjudication, prior-authorisation or reimbursement workflow.
- A universal outcome-based payment model across Abu Dhabi or the UAE.
- Independently measured reductions in avoidable claims, total cost of care or chronic-disease expenditure attributable to these platforms.

## 6. Monitoring agenda — 2027

1. **Platform operations:** look for official evidence of PHI production use, participating providers, population coverage, data sources and operational refresh cycles.
2. **Interoperability:** monitor any explicit technical or governance announcement connecting EDW/Bayan with PHI or emirate-level HIEs.
3. **Payer participation:** identify named insurers or TPAs participating in prevention, population-health or value-based-care programmes, and the legal basis and scope of their data access.
4. **Financing:** track clinical-costing submission coverage, reimbursement changes, provider contracts and any documented link between quality/outcomes and payment.
5. **Outcomes:** seek baseline and follow-up evidence on avoidable utilisation, repeat testing, chronic-disease outcomes, provider costs and claims expenditure.

## 8. Research conclusion

The UAE is assembling important components of a data-enabled and prevention-oriented health system. The strategic opportunity lies in whether those components become connected to clinical workflows, provider-cost measurement and insurance financing.

**Current assessment:** enabling infrastructure and policy direction are evidenced; an integrated predictive-insurance operating model and measurable insurance savings are not yet evidenced.

This signal should be updated only when new primary evidence demonstrates operational integration, payer participation, financing changes or attributable outcomes.
