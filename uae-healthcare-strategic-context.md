## 1. Institutional platforms and ownership models

### PureHealth — Care & Cover

PureHealth describes its model through two strategic verticals: **Care** (healthcare services) and **Cover** (insurance). Its public company overview describes the group as a vertically integrated payer-provider platform, with UAE and international operations.

- Abu Dhabi Media Office announced the IPO process on 25 November 2023.
- PureHealth was listed on the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange on 21 December 2023.
- The Abu Dhabi Media Office reported the IPO raised AED 3.62 billion.
- PureHealth's own investor-relations material describes its Care & Cover model.

**Interpretation to test:** The combination of payer and provider assets may create opportunities for coordination, operating efficiencies and different approaches to care financing. It may also create governance, competition and payer-provider incentive questions. These are hypotheses, not established outcomes.

- https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/economy/purehealth-listed-on-abu-dhabi-securities-exchange/

### M42 — technology-enabled integrated healthcare

M42 was announced on 19 April 2023 by G42 and Mubadala. The entity brought together G42 Healthcare and Mubadala Health, combining healthcare facilities and services with data-centric and technology capabilities. M42 describes its focus around AI, technology, genomics, precision, preventive and predictive care.

**Distinction:** M42 and PureHealth are separate groups with different corporate histories and portfolios. They should not be presented as one organisation or as interchangeable models.

Sources:

### Public healthcare delivery

Emirates Health Services (EHS) is a federal healthcare service provider. It belongs in the service-delivery/institutional landscape, not in the same category as health regulators. SEHA is part of PureHealth's provider portfolio; preserve this ownership distinction when mapping entities.

## 2. Regulatory architecture

The UAE health system includes federal and emirate-level authorities. At minimum, the observatory should distinguish:

- **MOHAP:** federal health-policy and ministry role.
- **Department of Health – Abu Dhabi (DoH):** regulator and strategic authority for Abu Dhabi's healthcare sector, including health insurance.
- **Dubai Health Authority (DHA):** health-sector authority for Dubai.

Jurisdiction is a required field in every regulatory signal. A Dubai circular must not be generalised to all UAE markets without corroborating evidence from other jurisdictions.

Sources:
- https://www.mohap.gov.ae/
- https://www.doh.gov.ae/
- https://www.dha.gov.ae/

## 3. Healthcare financing and mandatory insurance

Health insurance arrangements differ by emirate and population.

- Abu Dhabi has a long-standing mandatory health-insurance framework. DoH materials describe financing through employers/sponsors, government and individuals, and identify Thiqa as a government-funded scheme for eligible UAE nationals administered by Daman and regulated by DoH.
- Dubai has its own health-insurance framework and employer obligations.
- From 1 January 2025, the federal basic health-insurance scheme was extended to private-sector employees and domestic workers in the emirates where employer-provided cover was not previously mandatory, with the policy linked to residency-permit issuance or renewal. Existing permits are subject to the transition/renewal rules described by MOHRE.

**Important:** Do not flatten these arrangements into a claim that one identical insurance product or one identical rule applies to every resident in every emirate. Record the jurisdiction, covered population, effective date, employer/sponsor responsibility, package and exceptions separately.

## 4. Digital health-data infrastructure

The UAE has connected distinct health-information exchange platforms:

- **Riayati:** national unified medical record programme/platform.
- **Malaffi:** Abu Dhabi health-information exchange.
- **NABIDH:** Dubai Health Authority health-information exchange.

MOHAP announced the integration of Riayati, Malaffi and NABIDH on 31 January 2023. MOHAP's Riayati information describes the national record as connecting more than 3,000 healthcare facilities.

**Critical distinction:** Clinical health-record exchange is not the same as insurance claims exchange. The observatory must separately track clinical interoperability, claims transactions, payer-provider administration, coding, audit and payment processes. Access to clinical records is governed and must not be assumed to be generally available to insurers or TPAs.

## 5. Prevention, genomics and precision medicine

The UAE National Genome Strategy and Emirati Genome Programme are part of the country's move toward genomics, precision medicine and preventive healthcare.

In a UAE Cabinet update published in June 2024, the government reported that the Emirati Genome Programme had collected more than 600,000 samples at that point, and described the National Genome Strategy as supporting an integrated ecosystem for precision medicine and personalised healthcare.

**Evidence date matters:** the 600,000-sample figure is a dated 2024 milestone, not a current 2026 total. Newer figures and governance developments must be checked before reuse.

## 6. Healthcare industrialisation and local production

This is a relevant research domain, but the material collected in the current discussion is not yet sufficient to state a verified, joined-up national healthcare-industrialisation programme or quantify its results.

Research tracks to verify separately:
- pharmaceutical manufacturing and local production capacity;
- medical devices and diagnostics manufacturing;
- biotechnology and life-sciences investment;
- procurement, supply-chain resilience and medical logistics;
- industrial zones, incentives, tenders and actual facility investments.

Do not use broad industrial strategies as proof of a specific healthcare investment unless the source explicitly connects the programme, company, project and healthcare activity.

## 7. Workforce, education and clinical capability

This is a relevant research domain, but the current discussion has not yet established a sufficiently sourced national workforce instrument or measured outcome.

Research tracks:
- licensing and scope-of-practice rules by authority;
- residency, fellowship and specialist training capacity;
- workforce localisation and recruitment programmes;
- health-management, insurance operations, coding and clinical-data skills;
- dated vacancies and employer requirements, sampled separately from policy statements.

Do not infer job creation or hiring demand from a strategy announcement alone.

## 8. International positioning and medical tourism

International patient services, medical tourism, global hospital investments and cross-border partnerships may connect healthcare delivery to trade, investment and health-insurance markets.

The current discussion has not yet assembled enough primary evidence to state a unified UAE medical-tourism strategy or compare emirate performance. Research Dubai and Abu Dhabi separately, and distinguish:
- inbound patient volumes and spending;
- government promotion and accreditation programmes;
- provider investments and international partnerships;
- clinical specialities and source markets;
- published targets versus achieved results.

## 9. Initial cross-sector questions for the observatory

These are hypotheses to test against future signals, not conclusions:

1. Does payer-provider integration change network design, claims governance, cost management or competition?
2. Does the expansion of mandatory insurance change demand for basic products, provider networks, TPAs or claims operations?
3. How do clinical-data interoperability and claims-exchange systems interact, and where do their boundaries remain?
4. Do investments in prevention and genomics change payer incentives, benefit design or long-term cost patterns?
5. Which local manufacturing, procurement or supply-chain developments create measurable changes for providers and insurers?
6. Do international acquisitions and medical-tourism flows create new cross-border operating or insurance relationships?
7. Do policy changes and technology deployments translate into observable changes in employer requirements and workforce demand?

## 10. Evidence and interpretation rules

- Preserve the original source and publication date.
- Record event date separately from publication and observation dates.
- Label jurisdiction and affected population.
- Separate verified facts from company claims, government aspirations, analyst interpretation and hypotheses.
- Use primary sources for legal, regulatory and programme claims wherever possible.
- Do not treat a press release's stated ambition as proof of achieved impact.
- Add counter-hypotheses and follow-up tests to material interpretations.
- AI may help discover and connect evidence; human review remains responsible for verification and final judgement.
