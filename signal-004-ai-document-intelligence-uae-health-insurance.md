## 1. External Signal Detection

PureHealth's 9M 2025 Management Discussion and Analysis reports that Daman deployed an AI-powered Document Intelligence Platform in Q3 2025. The disclosed application concerns document classification, validation and data extraction at a stated scale of more than 60 million documents annually.

A subsequent PureHealth announcement identifies PureCS, a PureHealth subsidiary, as the developer and describes the solution as using large language models (LLMs), intelligent classification and structured data extraction. The announcement says the platform is integrated into Daman workflows that include enrolments, endorsements and complex claims.

This is an institutional deployment signal: it documents AI being integrated into health-insurance operating workflows. It does not, on the evidence cited here, establish autonomous claims adjudication or the transfer of final insurance decision authority to an AI system.

### B. Boundary of the evidence

The public materials establish document-processing automation. They do **not** establish that the platform autonomously approves, rejects or adjudicates health-insurance claims, determines medical necessity, or makes final coverage or payment decisions.

The PureHealth announcement refers to future AI-driven enhancements for claims and prior-authorisation processing. This future-oriented wording is not proof that those enhancements are already deployed.

The announcement identifies PureCS as technology developer and Daman as the insurance operation using the solution. It does not identify:

- a named operational owner for this specific use case;
- a formally accountable role for day-to-day operation;
- who may approve or reject consequential outputs;
- who may override, suspend or stop the system or its outputs;
- a documented escalation route or RACI for the platform;
- the model-risk, audit or performance-monitoring owner for this use case.

Executive commentary about strategic benefits does not constitute a delegation-of-authority instrument and must not be used to infer that an executive is the operational owner or decision authority.

**Finding:** the Daman AI document-processing deployment is publicly evidenced. The use-case-level ownership and decision-authority chain remains publicly unverified in the sources reviewed.

### Core analytical distinction

**Task automation ≠ decision authority ≠ accountability.**

An AI system can classify, validate and extract information without being authorised to make a final insurance decision. A technology developer, operating insurer, workflow team and accountable decision-maker may be different actors.

The research must establish three separate layers:

1. **Task execution:** what the platform actually performs in production.
2. **Decision authority:** who is authorised to accept, reject, approve, override or stop an output or downstream decision.
3. **Accountability:** which institution and designated human roles remain responsible for the workflow and its outcomes.

## 4. Impact & Opportunity Monitoring

### What is evidenced

- Daman's deployment of an AI-powered Document Intelligence Platform was reported for Q3 2025.
- The disclosed functions include document classification, validation and data extraction.
- PureHealth identifies PureCS as the developer.
- The company describes integration into Daman workflows including enrolments, endorsements and complex claims.
- PureHealth reports a scale of more than 60 million documents annually.
- Public organisational clues indicate relevant medical-automation, medical-intelligence, governance and AI product functions, but do not identify the owner of this particular use case.

### What is not established

- Autonomous claim approval, rejection or adjudication by the platform.
- Autonomous medical-necessity or coverage decisions.
- Production deployment of the future claims and prior-authorisation enhancements mentioned in the company announcement.
- The named operational owner or formally accountable role for this specific platform.
- Authority to approve, reject, override, suspend or stop the platform's outputs.
- The platform's specific human-review checkpoints, escalation paths, audit arrangements or performance outcomes.
- Independent confirmation that the stated efficiency targets have been achieved.

### Opportunity hypotheses to test

- Document-intelligence deployment may create operational needs around exception handling, data-quality review, workflow ownership and monitoring.
- The separation between technology development and insurer operations may make clear accountability, vendor oversight and escalation design important.
- If AI outputs feed consequential claims decisions, the boundary between document-processing assistance and decision support may become a material governance question.
- Roles spanning Medical Automation, Medical Intelligence, Claims Operations, IT, Compliance, Risk and AI Product may hold parts of the operating model; the actual allocation must be evidenced rather than inferred.

These are hypotheses, not established hiring demand, realised productivity gains or proof of a change in decision authority.

### Follow-up monitoring tests — close the ownership gap

1. **Identify the operational owner:** seek a Daman policy, operating procedure, model inventory, system owner record, RACI or named accountable role that explicitly refers to the Document Intelligence Platform or its defined use case.
2. **Map decision rights:** establish who may accept, reject, correct, override, suspend or stop outputs, and whether those powers differ by workflow (enrolment, endorsement, complex claims).
3. **Trace the human checkpoints:** determine where human review occurs, which exceptions are escalated, who resolves them and how decisions are recorded.
4. **Separate workflow stages:** map document intake, classification, validation, extraction, downstream recommendation and any final claim decision. Do not label the entire chain “automated adjudication” without evidence.
5. **Verify deployment scope:** seek dated operational documentation or attributable disclosures that distinguish live production functions from pilots, planned enhancements and vendor capabilities.
6. **Test performance claims:** seek a defined baseline, measurement period, denominator and independently attributable results for structured-data conversion, manual rework and processing time.
7. **Examine governance and vendor control:** identify the insurer's accountable owner, PureCS's contracted responsibilities, audit rights, incident escalation and system shutdown arrangements.
8. **Monitor workforce evidence:** compare dated vacancies and role descriptions for Medical Automation, Medical Intelligence, Claims Operations, AI Product, Compliance and Risk; treat job descriptions as organisational clues, not proof of platform ownership.

### Central research question

**Who owns the live AI document-processing workflow at Daman, and where does operational responsibility end and insurance decision authority begin?**

The observatory will not infer decision authority from the existence of automation, a job title, a vendor relationship or executive commentary. It will record that authority only when a source explicitly documents it.
